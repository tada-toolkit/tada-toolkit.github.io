Reading the front page ›

The headline window is labbed, not calculated: Furret_belle's late up tilt → up air chart for @MrZercon, patch 13.0, tested on Final Destination with no DI and no rage. On Final Destination at 0% rage you are reading their numbers exactly. On any other stage, platform or rage setting the window is shifted by the difference the knockback model says that change makes — so the labbed value stays the anchor and only the delta is computed.

Kills from is calculated throughout — the percent where each move first reaches the blast zone. Everything in Setups is labbed: ledge hang, Lloid spots and jab windows from the SSBU Isabelle Spreadsheet, pocket confirms from Zercon's guide, shield breaks from training mode.

Pocket and shield breaks ›

Pocket is intangible frames 5–23, stores on frames 8–23, pockets on frame 14, holds indefinitely, and throws on frame 9 for 1.9× the original damage.

Steals they cannot replace

These do not respawn while they are in your pocket, which is usually worth more than the damage you throw back: Link's Bomb (he cannot detonate it either), Wario's Bike, Diddy's Banana, R.O.B.'s Gyro, Wii Fit's Header Ball, Pac-Man's Fruit, Bowser Jr.'s Mechakoopa, the Belmonts' Holy Water, K. Rool's Crownerang and Banjo's Grenade Egg.

You cannot pocket Assist Trophies, Poké Balls, Smash Balls, anything those spawn, or projectiles with no detached model — Din's Fire is the standard example. Pikmin do not count as items here.

Jab ›

Frame 3 for 2%, total 19. It is +4 on hit at 0% and the advantage grows with percent, which is why its follow-ups are percent windows rather than flat guarantees — and why a heavier opponent waits longer for each one. BKB 14, KBG 30, Sakurai angle. An earlier version of this page read the base knockback of 14 as a fixed 14 frames of hitstun; the windows below could not move from character to character if that were true.

Lloid spots ›

The seven planting positions from Lath's chart. The front page gives the best spot per character and what it catches.

Spot 1
The very ledge, as far forward as possible.
Spot 1.5
From atop the ledge, roll away then dash in.
Spot 2
Back to the ledge, like after a roll or neutral getup. Joker's neutral getup doesn't work.
Spot 2.5
From atop the ledge, Ftilt offstage then Ftilt toward the stage — or walk one step forward.
Spot 2.8
Dash in, then late in the dash: dash away, buffer dash back in, Ftilt toward the stage.
Spot 3
Dtilt the ledge then turn — or dash away from atop the ledge and walk a step back.
Spot 4
From atop the ledge, roll away. After a neutral getup, walk toward the ledge first.

When in doubt, Spot 2. For characters where the ledge spot is good, plant there as well as the roll spot; for the rest the roll spot alone is usually better.

Ledge jump is the option Lloid struggles with — it catches the jump on almost nobody, and where it does, an airdodge still escapes.

Chart and spreadsheet by Lath, with AllToonedUp, PalmFire and Content, plus the Japanese Isabelle Discord. Updated October 2021, after the last balance patch — so it is current.

Stage data ›

The platform column is the height of each tier in world units, straight from the stage collision files. The chips under the headline run up tilt → up air from each of them: standing on a platform starts the opponent that much closer to the ceiling, so the confirm kills earlier. Small Battlefield genuinely has only two platforms and no top one. Town & City's and Kalos's heights shift with the stage phase — the listed values are the main ones.

Town & City has the shortest run from ledge to blast zone here, which is what a back throw character wants, and the highest ceiling, which is what a light floaty one wants. Pokémon Stadium 2 is the opposite trade. Smashville has the tightest ledges.

Small Battlefield carries Final Destination's blast zones, which SmashWiki documents as identical. Hollow Bastion reads straight from its collision file, trail_castle_00: Final Destination's blast zones exactly, ledges at 80, and one platform at 25. Northern Cave is still left out — no published values, and guessing them would put fake numbers in a calculator.

How the percents are made ›

KB = ( ( ( (p/10 + p·d/20) · (200/(w+100)) · 1.4 ) + 18 ) · s/100 + b ) · r

Ultimate's knockback formula: p is damage after the hit, d the move's 1v1 damage, w the target's weight, b base knockback, s knockback scaling, r rage.

Launch distance scales with the square of knockback, so the KO threshold for any distance is K·√distance. Calibrating on two published numbers — up smash KOs middleweights around 110% from the floor, back throw KOs heavyweights around 150% — gives K = 16.54 vertically and 16.29 horizontally. Different moves, angles and weights, agreeing within 1.5%.

Every character then differs by exactly one term: their weight. Not modelled: DI, staling, per-character gravity, platforms, and angle differences within a direction.
